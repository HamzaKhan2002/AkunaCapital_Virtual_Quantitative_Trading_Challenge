## KNOWN FAILURE MODES (from research)

- **Ignoring noise ($\sigma = 0$ approximation)**: Wrong prices, especially near-the-money. (Luthra explicitly flagged this for 2025.)
- **No inventory management**: Accumulates extreme positions → bankruptcy risk.
- **Static spread formula**: Fails on hard adaptive-bot scenarios.
- **Copy-pasting 2025 `expiry_valuation`**: `max(S-K, 0)` is wrong for binary options — must return 1 or 0.
- **Assuming independence across underlyings**: If joint options exist, pricing them as products of marginals is wrong.
- **Treating binary Δ like vanilla Δ ∈ [0,1]**: binary delta is unbounded near expiry ATM; blind delta-hedging there guarantees churn losses — cap hedges, cut size, widen quotes instead.
- **Assuming independence across options on the SAME underlying**: $\sum Q_i^2 V_i(1-V_i)$ misstates portfolio risk; same-underlying binaries are functionally dependent through $S_T$ (use covariances or the exact strike-partition payoff distribution).
- **Ignoring ties**: grid-rounded or $\sigma=0$ terminal prices give $P(S_T = K) > 0$; a wrong `>` vs `≥` convention shifts all near-strike prices systematically.
- **Ignoring the floor at 0**: breaks martingale + noise-sum near $S \approx 0$ — the Fed funds rate is the at-risk underlying.
- **Copying Luthra's near-expiry hacks** (Γ := 1 for T < 3): use analytic Greeks instead — they are exact for all $T \ge 1$ when $\sigma > 0$.

- **THE CHAOS DOCTRINE (E18)**: fixed-seed grader runs cannot rank strategy variants — fills are threshold events, sessions are iterated discontinuous systems, single-run per-test deltas have undefined sign. Only expectation proofs + ≥500-session matched-seed arena A/Bs count as evidence. Selecting the best single-run score = survivorship bias = Round-2 regression.
- **Winner's-curse floor**: never quote w below our own σε(V) (≈0.07–0.09 ATM at 18-day warm-ups). The λ-book respects this by construction — that is WHY it works; do not narrow it.
- **The λ-book principle — PARTIALLY REVISED (E30, Run 10 evidence)**: the two halves that survive are (a) the extreme side is self-insuring, and (b) never quote a half-width below our own σε (≈0.07–0.09 ATM at 18-day warm-ups). The half that is **REJECTED** is "proportional shading in distance-to-boundary space": bid edge p(1−λ) / offer edge (1−p)(1−λ) is an *unconditional* capital charge that makes us the cheapest counterparty on precisely the side with the smallest lock — i.e. the side that pays out against us most often (1.2¢ to sell a 0.90 option, 0.6¢ to buy a 0.05 one). That is self-inflicted adverse selection and it is why we lost money in *benign* sessions. Replacement: symmetric base edge + σε floor + a capital charge that only activates when capital is actually scarce (CAPITAL_WIDEN·utilization·lock). Detection thresholds must still work at 2–4 events/day (single-fill evidence, not EWMAs).
- **Quote SIZE is a first-class lever, not an afterthought (E30)**: on the grader's $10–40 books, a 6% per-quote lock budget yields literally 1 lot. Winning the price and showing 1 lot hands the profitable remainder to the next-best quoter via spillover — we become a loss-leader for the fixed-width bots. Always sanity-check realized quote sizes against the VERBOSE logs.
